# The Chrysalis Challenge

Complete one module. Fix one real weakness. Help one other person.

Project Chrysalis is free, public, and designed to be useful even if you only complete one part of it. The Chrysalis Challenge is a simple way to turn the audit into action.

## How to participate

1. Open the [Master Checklist](../worksheets/master-checklist.md)
2. Pick one module
3. Complete as much of that module as you can
4. Fix at least one real weakness
5. Share one lesson learned without private details
6. Send the audit to one person who would not have found it on their own

## Challenge levels

| Level | Requirement | Public-safe proof |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | Complete the Master Checklist self-assessment | Share one lesson learned, no private details |
| Aware | Complete Module 1 | Share account count only, not account names |
| Hardened | Complete Module 3 | Share before and after counts, such as SMS 2FA reduced from 7 to 2 |
| Resilient | Complete Modules 1, 2, 3, and 8 | Share one general weakness you fixed |
| Family Shield | Help one other person complete one module | Share an anonymized lesson learned |

## What not to share

Do not post or submit:

- Passwords
- Recovery codes
- Account names tied to your identity
- Screenshots of private account pages
- Email addresses
- Phone numbers
- Carrier PINs
- Home addresses
- Dependency graphs showing your real recovery paths

The point is to get safer, not to expose private details for social proof.

## Safe things to share

- A lesson learned
- A general category you improved
- A count without names, such as "I found 31 old accounts"
- A habit you changed
- A family verification rule you created, without revealing the phrase
- A reminder for others to complete one module

## Suggested public post

I completed one module of Project Chrysalis, a free personal digital security audit.

My biggest lesson: [one safe lesson]

One thing I fixed: [general category, no private details]

If you only do one thing this week, open the checklist and complete one module: https://chrysalis.buildbeyondbelief.com

## Prize philosophy

People should not have to prove private security work to benefit. If prizes are available, verification should be lightweight and privacy-preserving.

Acceptable verification:

- Self-attestation for low-value prizes
- One general lesson learned
- Optional redacted proof only when truly necessary
- Random selection among eligible participants rather than judging who is most secure
- Public winners share lessons learned, not sensitive proof
